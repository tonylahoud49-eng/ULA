## Container Inspection — CSLU 7234561 (40'HC)

### External Condition

The container was examined externally on 17 July 2026 at Warehouse 7. The following was noted:

- Container generally in fair condition for its age.
- **Roof panel:** A dent/depression approximately 40 cm × 25 cm was observed on the roof panel near the front left corner. Paint chipping and light surface corrosion present around the dent.
- **Door seals:** Original shipper's seal SH-882341 was intact and matched the B/L. Rubber door gaskets showed wear but were in serviceable condition.
- **Floor and walls:** No visible holes or perforations from outside.
- **Ventilation ports:** Both upper side vents were open. Left-side lower vent housing showed minor impact damage.

### Internal Condition (Post-Destuffing)

Upon entering the container (already partially destuffed by the consignee on 14 July):

- **Water ingress evidence:** Standing water (approx. 2–3 mm depth) observed along the left side of the container floor, extending from the front bulkhead to approximately mid-container. Water staining and tide marks visible on the container walls up to approximately 15 cm from the floor.
- **Condensation vs. external ingress:** The location and pattern of water damage is consistent with external water penetration through the compromised roof panel rather than cargo sweat or condensation. The dent in the roof panel has created a depression where rainwater could accumulate and seep through micro-cracks in the roof seams. No evidence of condensation damage on the upper portions of cargo.
- **Dunnage:** The wooden dunnage and pallets on the left side of the container were wet and showed signs of prolonged moisture exposure.

## Damage Assessment — Individual Units

### Case 3 — CNC Milling Machine JXP-500 (S/N: JXP500-26-003)

- **Crate condition:** Bottom and lower sides of wooden crate wet. Crate structural integrity maintained.
- **Machine condition:** Surface rust on unpainted machined surfaces of the bed and saddle. Ball screw assembly shows light corrosion. Siemens 840D control unit: moisture detected on internal circuit boards (measured with moisture meter: 68% RH inside control cabinet). Way covers and chip guards show water staining.
- **Assessment:** Repairable. Requires complete disassembly of ball screw, re-grinding or replacement of corroded way surfaces, drying and testing of control unit, replacement of all greased bearings.
- **Estimated repair cost:** USD 12,000–15,000.

### Case 5 — CNC Milling Machine JXP-500 (S/N: JXP500-26-005)

- **Crate condition:** Similar to Case 3. Water pooling inside the crate.
- **Machine condition:** More extensive corrosion than Case 3, likely due to position closer to the water ingress source. Spindle housing shows pitting corrosion. Automatic tool changer mechanism stiff; rust on indexing pins. Control unit interior: visible water droplet residue on PCBs.
- **Assessment:** Repairable, but spindle unit may require full replacement.
- **Estimated repair cost:** USD 18,000–22,000.

### Case 7 — CNC Milling Machine JXP-500 (S/N: JXP500-26-007)

- **Crate condition:** Moderate moisture. Desiccant bags fully saturated.
- **Machine condition:** Light surface rust on bed ways. Control unit appears dry. Tool changer operational. Coolant lines intact.
- **Assessment:** Repairable with surface treatment and re-lubrication.
- **Estimated repair cost:** USD 5,000–7,000.

### Case 9 — CNC Milling Machine JXP-500 (S/N: JXP500-26-009)

- **Crate condition:** Minimal moisture; farthest from primary water ingress path.
- **Machine condition:** Very light surface discoloration on two exposed machined surfaces. No corrosion on control unit. All mechanical components appear functional.
- **Assessment:** Minor cleaning and re-preservation required.
- **Estimated repair cost:** USD 2,000–3,000.

### Case 11 — CNC Milling Machine JXP-500 (S/N: JXP500-26-011)

- **Crate condition:** Major structural damage — top and right side splintered. Multiple staples and bolts sheared. "THIS SIDE UP" arrow was oriented correctly.
- **Machine condition:** The machine has sustained severe impact damage:
  - Cast iron base fractured at two mounting points.
  - Spindle assembly knocked out of alignment (measured 0.4mm runout; specification is 0.003mm).
  - Automatic tool changer arm bent approximately 15 degrees; two tool pockets shattered.
  - Control panel HMI display screen shattered; front panel cracked.
  - Ball screw cover dislodged and deformed.
- **Assessment:** Likely beyond economic repair. Cast iron base fractures cannot be reliably welded to achieve original tolerance specifications. Spindle replacement alone would cost approximately USD 8,000–10,000. A replacement unit is recommended.
- **Estimated replacement cost:** USD 22,500.00 (full invoice value).

### Case 12 — CNC Milling Machine JXP-500 (S/N: JXP500-26-012)

- **Crate condition:** Received tilted approximately 70 degrees from upright. Skid base intact but stress marks visible. Impact marks on the floor side of the crate.
- **Machine condition:**
  - Machine shifted approximately 30 cm within the crate.
  - Four levelling bolts sheared at their bases.
  - Coolant reservoir cracked; approximately 8 litres of cutting fluid leaked inside the crate.
  - Way covers bent; one Y-axis cover detached.
  - Control unit intact but mounting bracket cracked.
- **Assessment:** Repairable. Requires new levelling bolts, coolant reservoir, way covers, and control unit remounting.
- **Estimated repair cost:** USD 8,000–10,000.

## Probable Cause of Damage

### Water Damage (Cases 3, 5, 7, 9)

The water damage is, on the balance of probability, attributable to **rainwater ingress through the compromised roof panel** of Container CSLU 7234561. The dent on the front-left roof panel created a point of accumulation and likely separated the roof seam sealant, allowing water to enter during rain events. The voyage duration was 26 days (17 June to 13 July) during the monsoon season, during which the vessel would have encountered rainfall.

This finding is supported by:
- The localized pattern of damage along the left side of the container.
- The absence of water damage on the right side or in the upper portions of cargo.
- The saturated desiccant bags indicating prolonged moisture exposure rather than a single event.
- The absence of similar damage in the second container.

### Impact Damage (Case 11)

The severe impact damage to Case 11 is consistent with the package being **dropped from height during cargo handling operations** (loading, transhipment, or discharge). The fracture pattern of the wooden crate, the cast iron base fractures, and the spindle displacement all indicate a sudden high-energy impact from approximately 1.5–2.0 metres.

### Tilting Damage (Case 12)

The tilting of Case 12 is consistent with **improper cargo securing or shifting during rough weather**. The tilt pattern suggests the crate was not adequately braced against the container wall, allowing it to shift and lean during vessel motion.

## Recommendations

1. **Damaged machines should not be powered on** until they have been properly inspected, dried, and serviced by an authorized technician.
2. Formal **repair quotations** should be obtained from the machinery manufacturer's authorized regional service center.
3. A **recovery/subrogation claim** against the carrier (COSCO) and/or the container supplier should be considered, particularly regarding the defective container roof panel.
4. The container provider should be formally notified of the defective container condition.
5. **Salvage disposal** of Case 11 should only proceed after underwriter approval.
